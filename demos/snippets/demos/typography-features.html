How 26 Letters Fill 1,000 Pages

  • Typography
  • 6 min read
  • 2 notes

Typography is the craft of arranging type so that reading takes no effort. A reader should notice the words and never the letters. Most of the work is invisible: the space between lines, the length of a line, the place where a long word breaks. When those are right, nothing stands out, and that is the whole point.

Small marks carry a great deal. The 1st edition of a book and its 2nd differ by two raised letters. Water is H2O and not H2O. A note1 sits above the line so it does not interrupt it. Emphasis can stack, as strong and stressed at once, and long words such as internationalization or electroencephalographic break with a hyphen when the line needs it.

“The details are not the details. They make the design.”

Charles Eames, quoted in Eames Design

72 Points to the Inch

Headings keep their numbers the height of capital letters, are never hyphenated, and balance their lines when they wrap. Body text uses old-style numerals, so 1,450 sits as quietly in a sentence as a word does.
