Starting a sourdough culture

What you need

A culture needs flour, water and a jar. Wholemeal rye is the most forgiving flour to start withRye carries more wild yeast on the grain than white wheat flour, so it becomes active sooner.. Use water that has stood for an hour if your tap water smells of chlorine.

Choose a jar that holds at least three times the volume of the mixture. The culture rises as it feeds and needs the room.

The first week

Days one to three

Mix 50 grams of flour with 50 grams of water and leave the jar loosely covered at room temperature. Each day, discard half and add the same amounts again. Expect bubbles by the second day and a sharp smell by the third.

Days four to seven

Activity often drops around day fourThe first burst comes from bacteria that do not survive as the mixture turns acidic. The yeast that replaces them takes a few more days to build up.. Keep feeding. By day seven the culture should double in volume within six hours of a feed.

If it does not, move the jar somewhere warmer. A shelf above a radiator or beside the oven is usually enough.

Keeping it going

At room temperature

Feed a culture kept on the counter once a day. Use it when it has risen and before it falls back, when it floats in a glass of water.

In the fridge

A culture kept cold needs a feed once a week. Take it out the night before you bake and feed it twice before use.

When something goes wrong

Grey liquid on top means the culture is hungry. Pour it off and feed. Pink or orange streaks mean it is spoiled: throw it away and start again with a clean jar.

A culture that smells of nail varnish has gone too long without food. Two or three feeds a few hours apart bring it back.
